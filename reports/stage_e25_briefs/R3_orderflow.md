## 1. Answer (at most 250 words)

Order-flow imbalance (OFI) is the best-documented short-horizon variable in microstructure, but almost all of its power is contemporaneous or lives at horizons of one second to a few price changes. Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov (2014) explain 65% of 10-second mid-price changes with OFI measured over the same 10 seconds; that is impact, not a forecast. On ES itself, OFI shocks die out within one second (Takahashi 2025, 2008-2013), and an RNN on ES level-II data loses skill between the next book event and 1 s (Dixon 2018). Deep OFI models peak at about two average price changes (Kolm, Turiel, Westray 2023). At one minute, Cont, Cucuringu and Zhang (2023) find negative out-of-sample R2 for every OFI model on US stocks 2017-2019; their positive annualized PnL (0.21 to 0.43) ignores trading costs and falls from 1 to 30 minutes. The published horizon-decay curve is steep and has shortened over time: NYSE order-imbalance predictability ran to 30 minutes in 1996, 10 in 1999, 5 in 2002 (Chordia, Roll, Subrahmanyam 2005). The one minute-scale futures result found is Euro Stoxx futures (2016-2019): out-of-sample R2 of 1.5-1.7% for the next binned return, with no P&L or costs. VPIN carries no incremental volatility information on ES after controls and is not directional (Andersen and Bondarenko 2014). At retail latency (100 ms to seconds) the queue-imbalance and microprice signals are gone. No source in this pass publishes a per-trade gross above the MES/MNQ hurdle at 1-30 minutes. Two weak, unsized candidates are listed. Data: MBP-10 or MBO, not owned; Databento Standard $199/month gives only 1 month of L2/L3 history, Unlimited $4,500/month gives all of it.

## 4. Negative evidence

- ES OFI shocks die out within one second (2008-2013). Quote: "most impacts dissipate quickly, with responses beyond one lag nearly negligible. This implies that market adjustments to order flow and return shocks occur within a second" (Takahashi (2025), arXiv:2508.06788, Sec. 5.3)
- 1-minute-ahead OFI forecasts have negative out-of-sample R2 for all models (US stocks 2017-2019); the positive PnL ignores costs. Quote: "Table 8 ... OS R2 -0.37 -0.10 -0.36 -0.10 -0.36 -0.10 ... the strategy ignores trading costs" (Cont, Cucuringu, Zhang (2023), Tables 8 and 11)
- Order-imbalance predictive horizon shrank from 30 to 5 minutes between 1996 and 2002 (NYSE); adj. R2 at 5 min fell from 2.61% to 0.28%. Quote: "There is significance at up to thirty minutes during 1996, up to ten minutes in 1999, and up to five minutes in 2002" (Chordia, Roll, Subrahmanyam (2005), Table 2)
- Deep-learning OFI forecasts have an effective horizon of about two price changes. Quote: "the effective horizon of stock specific forecasts is approximately two average price changes" (Kolm, Turiel, Westray (2023), abstract [secondary-only])
- ES level-II price-flip prediction decays already between the next event and 1 s. Quote: "the performance of the RNN decays as we increase the prediction horizon from the next book update event to 1s in the future" (Dixon (2018), Sec. 4)
- Forecast accuracy on LOB data does not imply tradeable signals. Quote: "their high forecasting power does not necessarily correspond to actionable trading signals" (Briola et al. (2024), abstract)
- VPIN (ES 2008-2010): no incremental predictive power for volatility; and it is a volatility, not a direction, measure. Quote: "we find no evidence of incremental predictive power of VPIN for future volatility" (Andersen and Bondarenko (2014b), abstract)
- Vendor ES example: imbalance R2 about 0.01 on one day, no costs, 500-trade markout. Quote: "it’s common to see useful features like this with R² ≈ 0.01" (Databento blog (vendor))

## 6. Prop compatibility

- Flat by 15:10 CT: both candidates are 1-5 minute holds inside RTH; compatible.
- DLL and trailing drawdown: per-trade risk is small, but trade counts are high (tens to hundreds per day for minute signals), so commission drag, not drawdown, is the binding issue.
- P06 news rule: OFI dynamics change sharply at macro releases (Takahashi 2025: "price impact rises, flow impact declines, return volatility spikes"); a minute-scale order-flow book would naturally skip release minutes, which also keeps it clear of P06.
- HFT ban and no VPS: every signal that works in the literature (queue imbalance, microprice, 1-s OFI, MBO queue position) needs sub-second reaction (Dixon 2018 assumes 100 us to 1 ms CME latency). Only minute-scale variants are admissible for a retail API trader; they are also where the evidence is weakest.
- Automation: the TopstepX/ProjectX API permits automation (brief section 3); L2 data entitlement through it not verified.

## 7. Overlap with tested mechanisms

- No order-book, OFI, VPIN, queue-imbalance or microprice member exists in the mechanism index; every Stage D/E screen used 1-minute OHLCV.
- D.1g C6 passive-execution queue model (closed, not run): the closest relative; the latency literature supports that closure for a retail trader.
- K1-vwap and other K1 members: price/volume on bars, not signed flow. R4 owns trade-side features (delta, TBBO); R3-C1 differs by needing book depth.
- K8 cross-market legs: price-based and multi-hour; R3-C2 uses lagged cross-asset order flow at 1 minute.
- E.12/E.20 ML routes (1-min bars, FAIL): an OFI model would add features they did not have, but the published 1-minute OS R2 being negative on stocks is a warning that more features do not rescue minute horizons.
