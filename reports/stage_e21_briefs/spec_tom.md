### Why entry at the close of day -2 is the papers' window, and what the alternative would be
- Close-to-close returns: the return of day d runs from the close of d-1 to the close of d. Holding the returns of days -1, +1, +2, +3 therefore means entering at the close of day -2 (the second-to-last trading day) and exiting at the close of day +3. This is what the prompt's "settlement of trading day -2" gives under MX numbering.
- The parenthetical "(two trade dates before the month's last)" gives entry at the close of day -3. That adds day -2's return. MX remark: "Arguably, the unusually high returns at the turn-of-the-month could be construed as beginning with day -2." (PDF p.12). Etula WP: "in more recent samples the market returns are abnormally high also on the last three trading days before the turn of the month" (PDF p.2). A wider window therefore has some support, but it is a different hypothesis from the cited one. Choosing between the 4-day and 5-day windows is the lead's decision and must be fixed before any bar is read. This reader proposes the papers' 4-day window (the first freeze text above).

### What the evidence says (facts for the lead; the verdict is the lead's)
- Window: every source read uses [-1, +3] with close-to-close returns. In MX numbering the prompt's "settlement of day -2 to settlement of day +3" is exactly that window. The parenthetical is the only conflict.
- Out of sample after L&S (1987-2005): the effect persisted (MX), with the VW difference falling from 0.17% to 0.14% across the two halves.
- After 2008 (cash indexes): three sources show decay to null. Plastun et al. find DJIA 2010-2018 start of month 0.03% against 0.05% on other days. Han et al. (abstract) find it gone after 2001. Choi et al. find VW 2000-2012 at t 1.36. Chen-Chua find it concentrated on day +1 after 1993. Two sources show persistence: Etula's S&P 1980-2013 has no post-2008 split, and Kayacetin 1994-2023 is unverified. No source read gives per-day basis points for 2010-2024 on US indexes. Plastun's 2010-2018 figures (3 bp against 5 bp/day, DJIA) are the only post-2010 US per-day numbers found.
- Small caps (RTY): MX and Choi et al. find the effect stronger equal-weighted and in small caps. No source read tests the Nasdaq-100.
