Prognostic Discussion for 6 to 10 and 8 to 14 day outlooks 
NWS Climate Prediction Center College Park, MD 
300 PM EDT Sat October 03 2026 
 
6-10 DAY OUTLOOK FOR OCT 09 - 13 2026 
 
There has been a notable change in the model guidance compared to yesterday  
regarding the forecast across western North America as models, particularly the  
0z ECMWF ensemble, depict a more amplified mid-level ridge across the  
northeastern Pacific during the 6-10 day forecast period. This results in a  
shift in the downstream pattern with a tendency toward more troughing near the  
West Coast of the CONUS and ridging in the East. The 6z GEFS has made a  
considerable adjustment toward the aforementioned pattern, and is in better  
alignment with the ECMWF/Canadian/GEFS-AI ensembles compared to the 0z run. The  
manual 6-10 day 500-hPa height blend depicts above-normal heights across most  
areas from the Great Plains to the East Coast, with slightly below-normal  
heights west of the Rockies. Anomalous troughing remains forecast across the  
Bering Sea tied to a recurving tropical cyclone of the Western Pacific  
resulting in a negative 150 meter height anomaly center in the manual blend and  
below-normal heights across the Aleutians and far western Mainland. Given  
stronger ridging over the northeastern Pacific compared to yesterday,  
above-normal heights are indicated across the southeastern Mainland and  
Southeast Alaska. Near- to above-normal heights are forecast across Hawaii. 
 
Probabilities for above-normal temperatures are increased across most of the  
CONUS tied to transient mid-level ridging. Across the West, the largest  
positive temperature anomalies are favored early in the period, followed by an  
eastward shift in the ridge axis and a resultant eastward progression of  
anomalously warm temperatures. Tied to this evolution, probabilities for  
above-normal temperatures exceed 60 percent across the Southwest and Four  
Corners, along with parts of the Midwest and Tennessee Valley. Probabilities  
for above-normal temperatures are weaker across portions of the Gulf Coast and  
southern Texas due to increased precipitation and cloud cover. Increased  
southerly mid-level flow ahead of deep troughing over the Bering Sea favors  
increased chances of above-normal temperatures over most of Mainland and  
Southeast Alaska, with near-normal temperatures more likely across northwestern  
Alaska. Above-normal temperatures are favored across Hawaii, consistent with  
the reforecast consolidation tool. 
 
A stationary front across the Southeast and Gulf Coast favors elevated odds for  
above-normal precipitation, with several inches of precipitation possible over  
some areas. Probabilities for above-normal precipitation decrease farther north  
along the East Coast. The trend toward more troughing near the West Coast  
results in many forecast tools now leaning toward above-normal precipitation  
across the West. This is tied to an increase in cyclonic flow as well as the  
potential for a tropical disturbance over the eastern Pacific to approach the  
Gulf of California toward the end of the period leading to enhanced moisture  
advection. Given the large change in the tools relative to yesterday, a broad  
area of near-normal precipitation is highlighted for much of the Interior West  
in the updated outlook. Above-normal precipitation probabilities are increased  
over portions of the Southwest and Lower Four Corners closest to the potential  
influx of tropical moisture. Above-normal precipitation is also favored over  
Alaska tied to the potential recurvature of Typhoon Choi-Wan over the West  
Pacific. Above-normal precipitation is slightly favored for Hawaii. 
 
The official 6-10 day 500-hPa height blend consists of 30% of Today's 6z GFS  
Ensemble Mean centered on Day 8, 40% of Today's 0z European Ensemble Mean  
centered on Day 8, and 30% of Today's 0z Canadian Ensemble Mean centered on Day  
8  
  
 
FORECAST CONFIDENCE FOR THE 6-10 DAY PERIOD: Average, 3 out of 5, Continued  
good agreement regarding enhanced precipitation along the Gulf Coast and  
ridging expanding into the East, offset by a large change in the tools across  
the western CONUS compared to yesterday. 
 
8-14 DAY OUTLOOK FOR OCT 11 - 17 2026  
 
During week-2, anomalous troughing is favored to persist across Alaska and the  
north-Pacific, with ridging expanding into the western and central CONUS,  
consistent with a positive phase of the Pacific-North American pattern (+PNA).  
Troughing initially favored over the West is predicted to lift out and move  
eastward, with the 0z ECMWF, 0z/6z GEFS, and 0z Canadian expanding mid-level  
ridging over the western CONUS later in the period. The pattern across the  
eastern CONUS is more uncertain; although ridging is predominately favored  
early in the period, the building ridge in the West supports more troughing  
downstream over the East which would be consistent with a +PNA. The 0z GEFS and  
GEFS-AI solutions continue to be the most robust with a downward trend in  
500-hPa heights across the East around the middle of the period, with the ECMWF  
ensemble also indicating a broadening trough by the end of week-2. The week-2  
manual height blend depicts below-normal heights across Mainland Alaska and the  
Aleutians, increasing in magnitude farther west. The varying pattern across the  
CONUS leads to near-normal heights over most areas, with a +30 meter positive  
height anomaly center over the Midwest, Ohio Valley, and Mid-Atlantic tied to  
ridging early in the period. Weakly above-normal heights are depicted across  
the northeastern Pacific, extending into parts of Southeast Alaska and the  
extreme northwestern CONUS. Near-normal heights are forecast for Hawaii. 
 
Above-normal temperature probabilities are increased over most of the CONUS  
during week-2 tied to generally weak mid-level flow, but with periods of  
increased ridging early in the period (East) and later in the period (West).  
Near-normal temperatures are more likely across the Northeast due to the  
potential for a period of troughing in the middle of the period. The 0z GEFS  
and GEFS-AI ensembles are most robust, indicating a period of below-normal  
temperatures, although the 0z ECMWF and 6z GEFS ensembles are relatively  
warmer, reducing confidence. However, the +PNA pattern would be consistent with  
enhanced troughing in the East supporting the lean toward near-normal  
temperatures and lower probabilities for above-normal farther south.  
Above-normal temperature chances are increased across eastern Alaska under  
enhanced southerly mid-level flow, with near- to below-normal temperature odds  
increased over the far western Mainland and the Aleutians. The reforecast  
consolidation continues to support a slight tilt toward above-normal  
temperatures across Hawaii during week-2. 
 
Odds for below-normal precipitation are elevated across the Pacific Northwest  
and northern California tied to enhanced ridging over the northeastern Pacific  
and northerly mid-level flow. The potential for an influx of moisture tied to a  
tropical disturbance over the East Pacific that may track near the Gulf of  
California tilts the odds to above-normal precipitation across the Southwest  
and Four Corners. Increased chances for above-normal precipitation remain  
forecast across the Gulf Coast and Southeast tied to a stationary front,  
although this feature may begin to weaken during the second half of the period.  
Elevated chances for above-normal precipitation extend throughout much of the  
eastern CONUS due to the potential for moisture to track northward, along with  
increasing frontal activity associated with troughing later in week-2.  
Near-normal precipitation is forecast across the Northern Rockies, much of the  
Great Plains, and into the Upper Midwest given increased uncertainty and a weak  
mid-level flow pattern. Troughing and surface low pressure favor enhanced  
chances for above-normal precipitation across Alaska, with the highest odds  
(greater than 50 percent) shifting east to the southeastern Mainland and  
Southeast Alaska. Slightly enhanced probabilities of near- or above-normal  
precipitation are forecast for Hawaii.  
 
The official 8-14 day 500-hPa height blend consists of: 35% of Today's 6z GFS  
Ensemble Mean centered on Day 11, 40% of Today's 0z European Ensemble Mean  
centered on Day 11, and 25% of Today's 0z Canadian Ensemble Mean centered on  
Day 11  
 
 
FORECAST CONFIDENCE FOR THE 8-14 DAY PERIOD: Below Average, 2 out of 5, due to  
decent model agreement early in the period, but with a more variable and  
transient pattern emerging over the CONUS with weak or inconsistent signals for  
temperature and precipitation.  
 
FORECASTER: Thomas Collow 
 
Notes: 
 
 
The notation for the categorical forecast indicated on the maps is  
the same as that in the tables: A-above   N-near normal   B-below  
  
The temperature map shows regions with > 33% chance of being warmer 
(orange, "A"), colder (blue, "B"), or close to (gray, "N") normal. 
Labels on the shaded lines give the probability (> 33%) of the more 
likely category (B or A). Probability of N is always < 40%. 
  
The precipitation map shows regions with > 33% chance of being  
wetter (green, "A"), drier (tan, "B"), or close to (gray, "N") 
normal. Labels on the shaded lines give the probability (> 33%) of 
the more likely category (B or A). Probability of N is always < 40%. 
  
In the southwest and other climatologically dry regions - there will  
be a greater than 33.3% chance of no precipitation and occasionally  
even a normal (i.e. Median) value of zero - especially during the dry  
seasons.  In such cases a forecast of near normal is effectively a  
forecast of little or no precipitation.  
  
The climate prediction center uses 1991-2020 base period means as   
reference in the climate outlooks. 
 
The next set of long-lead monthly and seasonal outlooks will be released on  
October 15.
