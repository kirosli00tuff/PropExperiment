## (2) Per member

### K5-fomc-01 (MGC, sign of the 13:00 to 13:04 CT post-statement move)
- **Strength: weak.** No published multi-day post-FOMC study of gold was found (4 searches). The evidence is a chain of analogs.
- **Direction: continuation, by analogy, with a disagreeing post-GFC source.** BKL, IJT and NW (abstract) point to continuation. AGLM's post-GFC tightening reversal points to reversal after hawkish shocks.
- **Horizon:** neither directly. The analogs cover 15 days (IJT, NW), 5/20/50 days (BKL) and 10 to 20 weeks (AGLM).
- **Magnitude:** none published for gold. AHV: the 5 to 10 minute adjustment is "threefold" the 5-minute one (2007-2020, GC 5-min), intraday only. Do not convert analog bond numbers into gold ticks.
- Reason: the 5-minute gold move on a statement mostly reads the policy surprise. A hawkish surprise raises real yields and the dollar, and gold falls (AHV; SCR abstract: metals fall about 0.5% per 10 bp surprise, immediately). A multi-day continuation would need the real-yield or dollar move to keep drifting. BKL find that long nominal yields keep moving with the surprise for 20 to 50 days after target changes (1989-2008, mostly after hikes). They also find "even larger effects ... in TIPS markets", which shrink when the sample ends before the crisis. NW (abstract only) list the US-dollar exchange rate among the assets with post-meeting drift. With gold moving against real yields (EH, correlation -0.82, which the authors say need not be causal), this implies continuation of the gold move. Against that, AGLM find that after the GFC, the rise in long yields after tightening shocks reverses within 10 weeks while easing shocks keep pushing yields down. That gives continuation after dovish moves, reversal after hawkish ones, and a general bias toward gold strength after any FOMC shock. All of this is nominal-yield evidence, not gold. AHV show continuation only from 5 to 10 minutes and call it a "short-term" inefficiency. That is within-day, but it is the only gold-specific post-FOMC result. Overall the mechanism is plausible but untested in gold. The lead should treat any expected-gross figure for MGC as unsupported by published numbers.

### K2-fomcpost-01 (ZT ZF ZN TN ZB UB, fixed BUY on FOMC statement days)
- **Strength: weak** for the member as written (fixed side, unconditional). The published unconditional post-FOMC Treasury estimates are not significant (CMVJ, PP). The strong drift results are conditional on the sign of the surprise, which this member does not use. A surprise-signed variant would rate moderate: BKL with t about 2 at 20 days for hikes but a pre-2010 sample; IJT post-GFC abstract only; AGLM post-GFC weekly.
- **Direction: same side as the member (BUY) weakly, with disagreement.** CMVJ, PP day +1 and H25 (via PP) favour BUY. AGLM post-GFC favours BUY for long maturities at 10 to 20 weeks, but not for 2Y after tightening (2Y yields stay up). BKL favours the opposite side (SELL) after hawkish surprises and about zero after cuts. So the unconditional sign depends on the sample's mix of hawkish meetings, and 2015-2018 and 2022-2023 were hiking cycles. PP also find that "the post-FOMC yield movement displays an upward trend between 2012 and 2016" (against BUY).
- **Horizon:** 5 days partly (CMVJ week 0 = days -1..3; PP day +1 only); 20 days only through CMVJ's even-week pattern (weeks 2 = days 9 to 13; not significant). AGLM speaks to 10 to 20 weeks, beyond 20 days.
- **Magnitude:** CMVJ, 10Y excess return 1994-2013: odd weeks 0.4 bp/day; week 0 is 2.4 bp/day higher (about 2.8 bp/day); weeks 2, 4, 6 are 1.3 bp/day higher. None is significant ("not statistically significantly higher ... at conventional confidence levels"). My own arithmetic, not published: days 1 to 3 of week 0 give about 8 bp of 10Y bond return per event, and the even-week excess over days 1 to 20 gives about 7 + 1.3 x 7 = 16 bp above the odd-week rate, both not significant. PP, 1994-2025: day +1 10Y yield -0.53 bp (t -1.12), 2Y -0.36 bp (t -0.96). Duration times 0.5 bp is a few bp of price, not significant. No decay figure is published. PP's 2012-2016 upward post-FOMC trend is the only sub-period note.
- Reason: two mechanisms bear on the member. (a) A risk premium earned around FOMC news. Long-yield declines concentrate in the days around meetings (H25 via PP, days -1..+1; PP show the significant part is day -1, before the member's entry). CMVJ find a weaker, insignificant bond version of the even-week equity pattern. Only day +1 and CMVJ's week-0 tail fall inside the hold. (b) Slow-moving capital after surprises. Bond-fund investors sell after salient hikes and arbitrageurs absorb the supply gradually (BKL; AGLM also point to "slow and persistent mutual fund flows"). This makes post-FOMC returns depend on the surprise sign: continuation, and SELL after hawkish surprises pre-2008 (BKL). Post-GFC, it is asymmetric toward lower long yields after both easing and tightening shocks (AGLM). A fixed BUY collects (a) and the AGLM asymmetry, and pays (b) on hawkish meetings. ZT (2Y) is the maturity where AGLM show no post-GFC reversal after tightening.

### K2-predrift-01 (ZN ZB, sign of the 08:30 to 08:49 CT move before ISM Services)
- **Strength: none: intraday-only.** The only documented mechanism is informed trading in the 30 minutes before the release (KSSW). It shows up as a pre-release move with the sign of the coming surprise and ends at the release. The only multi-day evidence on the release's effect (AGM) says the news-driven yield move persists but does not drift further.
- **Direction: neither continuation nor reversal predicted for the 5 to 20 days after the release day settlement.** AGM: news-driven moves are "persistent" (no reversal) and the news-driven component of returns is "unpredictable" (no continuation). Nothing found contradicts this for ISM Services.
- **Horizon:** neither. KSSW cover [t-30min, t+5min]. AGM cover daily changes aggregated to 22 and 66 days, with no post-release drift test.
- **Magnitude:** KSSW (2008-2014, 10Y note futures, intraday) give regression slopes on standardised surprises: [t-30min, t-5s] -0.058, [t-5s, t+5min] -0.041, so the pre-release share is 59%. These are slopes, not per-event P&L, and nothing is multi-day. I found no published figure for the 2019-2024 period or for any decay of the ISM pre-release drift [unverified either way].
- Reason: some traders know or forecast the ISM Services number before 10:00 ET. Their trading moves ZN/ZB in the "correct" direction about 30 minutes early. This accounts for 59% of the total 10Y move for ISM Non-Manufacturing, which passes a Holm correction at 1% (KSSW). Once the number is public, this information is fully in the price, so the mechanism predicts nothing after the release. AGM's decomposition finds that macro-news shocks to yields are persistent but that the part of bond returns driven by news "is unpredictable". The program's entry at the release-day settlement therefore has no published reason to earn anything over the next 5 to 20 days. Pre-FOMC context, in case the family label was intended: the 24-hour pre-FOMC window shows "no such effect in U.S. Treasury securities" (LM, 1994-2011), and IJT (abstract) also find no pre-FOMC drift in long bond futures. PP (1994-2025) find a significant day -1 10Y drift (-0.79 bp yield, t -2.39). All of this is before the announcement and says nothing about the 5 to 20 days after.
