## 2. Per-member paragraphs

### CP2 opening-range breakout port (K1 MNQ/M2K/MYM, K2 MES, K3 FX, K4 energy, K5 metals, K6 ags/livestock)
- Strength: none: intraday-only, for the ORB mechanism itself. The nearest multi-day analogs: moderate for equity index roots (reversal at 1-5 days) and weak for non-equity roots.
- Direction: the literature disagrees by horizon and asset class. Equity index roots, 5 days: reversal (BvBD19, BDLM21), and range breakouts held 10 days give no continuation (TSAI19, STW99). Non-equity roots, 20 days: weak continuation from the monthly analog only (MOP12). At 1-3 days it is reversal (BDLM21; not significant in FX).
- Horizon: 5 days for the equity reversal (daily and weekly AR(1), MAC(5)). 5 to 50 days for the breakout null: TSAI19 at 10 days (2003-2013), STW99 at 5, 10, 25 and 50 days (S&P 500 futures 1984-1996). 20 days only through MOP12's monthly analog. No source tests a one-day ORB signal over 5 or 20 days.
- Reason: Every ORB study read closes the position the same day (TSAI19 b; HLL13; LUN13; Crabel's articles are paywalled and were not read). Their mechanism is an intraday move that begins near the open: order flow, and for TAIEX institutional flow (TSAI19). Nothing in them predicts the days after the trigger. The E.23 entry is at the trigger day's settlement, so the question becomes whether a day whose early range break signals a trend day keeps trending. For equity indexes the evidence says no. Daily and weekly index returns have been negatively autocorrelated since about 1999, including in index futures, and the authors attribute this to price pressure from index arbitrage (BvBD19 a-d). BDLM21 finds that the late-day momentum such days carry reverses within two days in equity and commodity futures (BDLM21 b, c). Breakout rules held a fixed number of days, the direct multi-day form of a breakout, earned nothing on S&P 500 futures 1984-1996 (STW99 c, d), and their pre-1987 DJIA success decayed. On DJIA and S&P 500 futures 2003-2013 they were not significant at a 10-day hold (TSAI19 c, d); NASDAQ futures were significant over the full period but in neither half. For commodities, bonds and FX, MOP12 finds that a positive month predicts the next month (t 2.4-3.3), but a single trigger day is a small part of a month's return. In equity index futures that monthly effect is absent (t 1.05).
- Magnitude: BvBD19 S&P 500 daily AR(1) -0.076 (t -3.29) and weekly AR(1) -0.077 (t -1.64), 1999-2016; Nasdaq 100 -0.065 (t -2.79) and -0.074 (t -1.63); Russell 2000 -0.062 (t -2.52) and -0.065 (t -1.48). As an illustration only, not a published figure: a 100 bp trigger day implies about -7.6 bp the next day under the S&P AR(1). A MAC(5) contrarian strategy on the S&P 500 had Sharpe 0.67 after March 1999. TSAI19 gives no per-trade bp for the 10-day TRB in the text read. MOP12 gives t statistics, not per-trade bp. Decay: breakout profits decayed after 1986 (STW99). The index autocorrelation changed sign from positive to negative around 2000 (BvBD19).

### CP3 prior-close location port (same roots)
- Strength: weak (equity index roots). None (non-equity roots: no close-location evidence beyond BDLM21's 1-3-day hedging reversal).
- Direction: reversal (opposite to the member's side: CP3 buys after CLV >= 0.8). The evidence is that this reversal is a next-day effect that is gone before a 5-day hold starts. On IVV (S&P 500) the reversal after strong closes (CLV >= 0.8) is absent (PJ23 c).
- Horizon: 1 day for the CLV and IBS evidence (PJ23 d). For 5 and 10 days the only evidence uses the 5- or 10-day average of relative close (CXO20 c), not a single day's CLV. Nothing found for 20 days.
- Reason: CLV is the internal bar strength (IBS) statistic, and CP3 uses the same 0.2/0.8 cuts as Pagonidis (PJ23 a). The published reading is short-lived mean reversion: a close near the low is followed by a rise the next day, and a close near the high by a fall. The mechanism offered is the reversal of late-day price pressure. BDLM21 ties the late-day move to gamma hedging and finds that it reverts within 1 to 3 days in equity, commodity and bond futures (BDLM21 a-c). Its timing hurts the E.23 hold. CP3's event is day d-1's close location. The E.23 entry is at day d's settlement, one full session after the event, and PJ23 finds no effect beyond one day (PJ23 d). For the S&P 500, the next-day linear relation is nil (correlation 0.02; CXO20 b). After 2000 only weak closes reverse (CXO20 a), and IVV's short side after strong closes won only 46% of the time (PJ23 c). CXO20 c shows reversal at 5 and 10 days for low averaged relative closes. That result is descriptive and in sample, has no t statistic, and is not the CP3 statistic.
- Magnitude: no published bp or t statistic for a single-day CLV at 5 or 20 days. PJ23: IVV probability of a positive next day 0.566 after IBS < 0.2 and 0.460 for the short after IBS > 0.8; IVV threshold-strategy Sharpe 0.20; 2009-2019; next-day hold. Pagonidis (2013) reports an alpha above 30% a year before costs [unverified: search snippet only; the PDF host returned 522 and Wayback has no copy]. Decay: none documented. CXO20's full sample since 1962 shows no pattern, and the low-side reversal appears only after 2000.

### K1-vwap-01 (MNQ)
- Strength: none: intraday-only.
- Direction: no published multi-day direction. If the lead defines the trigger side as the position held into 14:58 (close above or below VWAP), the nearest analog is a strong late-day move, and that predicts reversal within 1-3 days (BDLM21, equity futures), plus next-day reversal of the index return (BvBD19). Both are analogs, not tests of this trigger.
- Horizon: neither 5 nor 20 days is addressed by any VWAP source found.
- Reason: The rule's published source is an intraday order-imbalance strategy on QQQ that holds no position overnight (ZA23 a). Its stated mechanism is trading with the intraday imbalance measured by price minus VWAP, which says nothing about later days. Three queries found no paper testing price relative to VWAP against next-day, 5-day or 20-day returns in index futures (query log rows 4, 18). For multi-day prediction, the only channel left is that a close above VWAP correlates with a strong late-session move, which BDLM21 and BvBD19 tie to reversal. That makes K1-vwap-01 a weaker, noisier version of the CP3 and CP2 equity case.
- Magnitude: none published for multi-day. ZA23's intraday figures are for QQQ 2018-2023: $25,000 to $192,656 net of commissions; Sharpe 2.1 on the authors' page.
