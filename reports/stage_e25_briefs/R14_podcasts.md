## 4. Negative evidence

- Gary Hart (BST 085), pointer: "day trading has gotten harder every year, particularly after quantitative easing started suppressing intraday volatility around 2011 and 2012. Low volatility environments are particularly brutal for intraday strategies because you need price movement to generate returns large enough to overcome commission and slippage." This is a decay claim that fits the program's cost-hurdle failures.
- Andrea Unger (BST 045), pointer: "For intraday trend-following entries on the S&P E-mini, Andrea says he hasn't been able to find reliable setup patterns." This fits the K1-cp1-01 and E20H6-T1 FAILs.
- Hart's cited record (Futures Truth) is hypothetical by that platform's own label (R7 E16, "tracks hypothetical performance"), so it is not a live record. The host's words "live trading results closely matching the backtest" are not backed by any document on the page.
- WCC records (Davey, Serafini, Unger) are contest returns from small, leveraged accounts, with only the top 5 published (R7 E11-E13). Selection bias: survivorship among roughly thousands of entrants, and the hosts pick guests for success stories.
- Prop outcomes: CWT 306 (prop CEO) and 262 (funded in 60 days) carry no pass, payout or style statistics on their pages. R8 owns prop outcome data.
