Frequently asked questions
What is the No Activation Fee path?
The No Activation Fee path is a Trading Combine® option that lets you get funded without paying an Express Funded Account (XFA) activation fee. You select this path when you start your Combine, trade under the same rules and objectives as always, and once you pass, your XFA activates for no additional cost.
Who is the No Activation Fee path best for?
This path is designed for traders who already show discipline and consistency in their trading. If you’re confident you can pass the Trading Combine® in one or two attempts, the No Activation Fee path gives you the lowest all-in cost to getting funded.
What's the difference between Standard and No Activation Fee?
Both paths use the same rules and objectives. The Standard Trading Combine® has a lower monthly subscription and requires a $149 XFA activation fee once you pass. The No Activation Fee Trading Combine® has a higher monthly subscription but your XFA activates for $0 when you pass.
How much do Resets cost on the No Activation Fee path?
Resets cost the same as your monthly Trading Combine® subscription for the account size you’re trading.
Can I switch paths later?
Yes. You can choose a different path any time you start a new Trading Combine®.

The Trading Combine, Express Funded Accounts and Topstep Labs experiments are realistic simulations of trading under actual market conditions and are difficult to pass even for experienced traders. Simulated performance results have certain limitations. Unlike an actual performance record, simulated results do not represent actual trading. Also, because the trades have not actually been executed, the results may have under- or over-compensated for the impact, if any, of certain market factors, such as lack of liquidity. Simulated trading programs, in general, are also subject to the fact that they are designed with the benefit of hindsight. Simulated trading does not involve financial risk, and no simulated trading record can completely account for the impact of financial risk in actual trading. For example, the ability to withstand losses or adhere to a particular trading program in spite of trading losses are material points which can also adversely affect actual trading results.
